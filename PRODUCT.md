# Product

## Register

brand

## Platform

web

## Users

People looking to book a wellness or activity appointment (massage, class, treatment) with Leviosa. They land on the public site to browse services and practitioners, then book — often on mobile, often making a first-time trust decision about a health-adjacent service.

## Product Purpose

The client-facing storefront where visitors discover Leviosa's services and team, then book an appointment. Success is a completed booking with minimal drop-off between landing and confirmation.

## Positioning

Booking as simple as Doctolib: pick a service, pick a person, pick a slot, confirm. The site's job is to get out of the way of that flow, not to sell a business decision.

## Conversion & proof

- Primary CTA: Book an appointment
- Secondary CTA: Browse services or team, for visitors not ready to commit
- Belief ladder: this feels credible and professional → this service/practitioner fits what I need → booking takes almost no effort → I'm comfortable giving my time and payment
- Proof on hand: team/practitioner bios (`(public)/team`); no external testimonials or press yet

## Brand Personality

Professional, confident, energetic. Trades spa-calm for street-culture energy — closer to a youth-facing streetwear ad than a wellness portal — while keeping the trust and professionalism a health-adjacent booking flow needs. Not clinical, not corporate-SaaS, not sedate.

## Anti-references

Nothing named explicitly. Avoid a cold, clinical, generic-healthcare-portal feel; avoid a generic SaaS-marketing look (this is a storefront for a practice, not a pitch to business buyers).

## Design Principles

- Booking flow simplicity above everything: fewest possible steps from "I want this" to "confirmed."
- Confident and energetic over sedate-and-spa or generic-corporate — closer to a streetwear ad than a wellness portal or a tech tool.
- Accessible by default: health-adjacent context means WCAG AA is a floor, not a stretch goal.
- Let the team carry the trust: practitioner bios are the primary proof point until reviews/press exist.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

WCAG AA minimum. Treated as core given the health-adjacent nature of the service, not optional polish — full keyboard and screen-reader support, sufficient contrast, and respect for reduced-motion preferences.
